Health inspections: official ones only

Pennsylvania has no statewide restaurant grade, and we don't invent one. Each agency's records stay its own: whether a Philadelphia place holds an active City food license (3,742 places), and links to the inspection searches the Department of Agriculture, Bucks and Montgomery counties and Philadelphia keep. Nothing is ranked by any of it.

What it costs: nothing

There's nothing to buy and nothing to sign up for, and there are no ads. The 23,421 places ship inside the app, so a list opens on a back road with no signal.

Where you eat stays with you

The app has no account and no analytics, and sends us nothing. Location, if you allow it, puts the nearest places first on your phone and goes nowhere else. Saved places live on your phone; on this website they live in your browser, which gets no cookies and no trackers. The privacy policy has the details.

Questions

How do restaurant inspections work in Pennsylvania?

There is no statewide restaurant grade. The Pennsylvania Department of Agriculture and about 100 local health departments inspect, and post results one search at a time; Philadelphia, Allegheny, Bucks and Montgomery counties keep their own. Allegheny County's Health Department posts a placard after each inspection: green, yellow "Consumer Alert" or red for a closure. For every other place, the app links the agency's own search; we never compute a grade. In the Department of Agriculture's words: "Please remember that any inspection is a 'snapshot' of the day and time of the inspection. An inspection conducted on any given day may not be representative of the overall, long-term cleanliness of an establishment. Also, at the time of the inspection, violations are recorded but are often corrected on site prior to the inspector leaving the establishment."

What's the difference between a cheesesteak, roast pork and a hoagie?

A cheesesteak is griddled, thin-sliced beef with cheese on a long roll; roast pork is thin-sliced pork, usually with sharp provolone and greens; a hoagie is the cold sandwich of Italian meats and provolone on a seeded roll. All three are on the hand-checked Cheesesteaks list.

Where do the lists come from?

From our own research in October 2026: every place on the cheesesteak, tomato pie, Pennsylvania Dutch and other lists has a dated 2025 or 2026 source, such as a newspaper or tourism guide, or its own website. The rest of the 23,421 restaurants come from Overture Maps' open place data and the City of Philadelphia's food licenses.

What about MICHELIN stars and James Beard awards?

They're in, as facts: "1 MICHELIN Star", "Bib Gourmand" or "Green Star" from the MICHELIN Guide's first Philadelphia selection (November 2025), and James Beard awards and America's Classics from 2025 and 2026, as the foundation named them. They're never shown as a row of stars.

Why are there no star ratings?

No list here is ordered by ratings, and the app keeps none, ours or anyone else's. The "Ratings, hours & photos" button on a place opens Apple Maps' live card, and whatever that card shows is Apple's.

Does the app track where I am?

No. If you allow location, it's used on your iPhone or iPad (or, on this site, in your browser) to put the nearest places first, and it's never sent to us. Say no and everything but Near me still works.

Spotted a closed shop or a missing pierogi kitchen?

Write to nick@eatsranked.com with the place's name and its town. Fixes ship with the next data update.

Does it cost anything, and is there an Android version?

It costs nothing: no price, no subscription, no ads and no account. The app is for iPhone and iPad for now; on Android, the web search on this site has the same places and lists.
